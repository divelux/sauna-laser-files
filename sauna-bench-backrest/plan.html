How it stands

Each side is a triangle: a base rail on the bench, a stile leaning back 12°, and a brace from the stile down to the back of the base rail. The back ends of the base rails go against the wall, so the wall takes your weight when you lean back; the triangle keeps the angle. The stiles do not touch the wall.

Your bench must be at least 20 in (508 mm) deep: 10 in (254 mm) for the base and room to sit in front of it.

Backrest: assembly

Two triangle frames and seven slats, screws hidden at the back

Assembly

  1. Cut all parts. Round over every edge you can touch, the slats on all four long edges, and sand.
  2. Cut the foot of each stile at 12° from square, so it stands flat on the bench while leaning back.
  3. Lay out one side flat, outside face down: base rail, then the stile on top of it with the front of its foot 1 in (25 mm) from the front of the base, leaning back 12°. Screw the stile to the base rail with two screws from the stile side.
  4. Put the spacer on the base rail at the back end. Lay the brace from the top of the spacer to the stile; mark both ends, cut them so the brace stays behind the front face of the stile and above the bench. Screw the brace to the stile (two screws) and through the spacer into the base rail (two screws).
  5. Build the second side as a mirror image. The screw heads go on the inner faces, the outer faces stay clean.
  6. Stand both sides up, 20 in (508 mm) apart outside to outside. Screw the top slat on first, 22-3/4 in (578 mm) up the stile, then the bottom one, then the rest with 1/2 in (13 mm) gaps. Drive the screws from the back of the stiles into the slats.
  7. Check the diagonal: the frame should not rack. Check every surface by hand. Leave it unfinished.

Wood

Use a wood that is sold for sauna interiors where you live, such as aspen, alder or western red cedar. Choose clear boards without loose knots or resin pockets. The stiles and the brace carry the load: pick straight grain for them.

Safety

  • Build at your own risk. These are hobby plans, not engineered drawings, and nobody has checked them for your bench, your wall or your sauna.
  • Check that nothing can burn or pinch: no metal on a surface skin can touch, no sharp edges, no gaps that can trap a finger.
  • Round over or sand every edge you can touch. Leave wood for the hot room unfinished: no varnish, paint or stain.
  • Hot room or not: screws and other metal get hot in a sauna. Keep every head below the surface or on the hidden side.
  • Always set it with the base rails against the wall. Away from a wall it tips backward when you lean on it.
  • Do not stand on it or use it as a step.
